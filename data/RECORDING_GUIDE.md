# Recording guide: six natural code-mixed Hindi clips (Part D)

**Status: no recordings exist yet.** The pipeline never generates or substitutes audio for these
clips. Until you record them, every timing-dependent number is computed from *placeholder*
timings; every result row is tagged `placeholder_estimate` and the plots/report label seconds as estimated.

## Before you record

- Quiet room, phone or laptop microphone 15–25 cm from your mouth, no fan or AC noise.
- **Audacity (recommended):** set Project Rate to 48000 or 24000 Hz, mono, record, then
  *File → Export → Export as WAV* (signed 16-bit PCM). Save as `data/audio/raw/clip01.wav`, etc.
- **Phone recorder:** most phones save `.m4a`. Either install ffmpeg
  (`winget install Gyan.FFmpeg`, then open a new terminal) or open the file in Audacity and export WAV.
- One speaker per clip (Sarvam §3). You are the only speaker; `speaker_id = spk-0001`.
- Consent: you are recording yourself. The names, numbers and account digits in the prompts are
  invented (the phone number uses the well-known dummy pattern 98765 43210/8). Do not substitute
  real personal data.

## How to speak

These are **prompts, not a script to read word for word**. Speak the content as you naturally
would on a phone call: pauses, "अं", repeats and small rewordings are welcome; that is what makes
the clip natural. Then edit the transcript so that it matches the audio exactly (see "After
recording" below).

Each clip must be **20–40 s long**. At a natural pace the prompts take about 22–28 s. If a clip is
under 20 s, add a sentence or speak more naturally; don't pad it with silence.

Gendered verb forms (बोल रहा/रही हूँ, करूँगा/करूँगी, सकता/सकती) should be whatever is natural for
you. The transcript must match what you actually said.

Required coverage across the six clips: amounts in lakh and crore, a phone number, names,
negations, self-corrections, and natural Hindi–English code-mixing.

## Recording checklist (tick per clip)

- [ ] 20–40 s, one take, no edits that cut through words
- [ ] no clipping (the Audacity meter never hits 0 dB); speech peaks around −12 to −6 dBFS
- [ ] the self-correction is spoken as a *real* correction (hesitate, then correct)
- [ ] numbers spoken as words (not "nine-eight-seven" in English unless that is what you say;
      if you do say English number words, the transcript must contain them in Devanagari:
      नाइन एट … and the checker annotation must be updated)
- [ ] file saved as `data/audio/raw/clipNN.wav`

## After recording (the audio is the source of truth)

1. `python scripts/prepare_audio.py` converts to mono 16-bit 24 kHz and checks duration and level.
2. Listen to each clip and edit `data/annotations/clips.json` so that every `hi_text` matches
   exactly what you said, in IndicVoices Level 1 style ("transcribed as spoken"):
   - disfluencies, repetitions and fillers included (e.g. "अं", "मतलब", repeated words);
   - English words in Devanagari as spoken (ऑफिस, not "office" and not "ऑफिस (office)");
   - numbers as spoken words (पाँच करोड़, not 5 करोड़);
   - every sentence ends with sentence-final punctuation (। or ?).
   If you changed wording, update `en_literal`/`en_fluent` and the entity `src_phrase`/`en_phrase`
   fields to match. `python scripts/validate_dataset.py` tells you what no longer matches.
   Then set `status.transcript` to `"verbatim_verified"` and `status.translation` to `"human_reviewed"`.
3. `.venv\Scripts\python scripts/run_alignment.py` runs the forced aligner.
4. `python scripts/audit_boundaries.py make-sheet`, then mark the 30 word onsets by hand in
   Audacity (instructions are in that script) **without** importing the automatic labels first,
   then `python scripts/audit_boundaries.py score`.
5. `python scripts/run_all.py` regenerates every result, RESULTS.md and the PDF report.
